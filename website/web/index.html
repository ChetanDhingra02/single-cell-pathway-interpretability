A single-cell interpretability study

Can we trust pathway labels inside a neural model?

Some neural models give hidden units the names of biological pathways. I tested whether those pathway-labelled units are actually driven mainly by genes from the pathway on their label.

Pathway-constrained autoencoders assign gene-set names to latent units. I audit whether those labels are faithful using donor-held-out cross-validation, attribution and ablation analyses, pathway-overlap analysis, and independent HIRISA validation.

Plate 01 · The question

A pathway name is a claim, not a guarantee

Each latent unit, shown here as a dial, is labelled with a pathway. Its decoder may only reconstruct that pathway’s genes, but its encoder can use any input gene. The label says what the unit may reconstruct, not necessarily what drives it.

Model D uses an unrestricted encoder and a masked, non-negative decoder. Latent unit k can reconstruct only genes assigned to pathway k, but any input gene can contribute to the encoder calculation of that unit.

So does the IFN-α unit depend mainly on IFN-α pathway genes, or does it also rely strongly on genes outside its label?
Plate 02 · How we tested

Three faithfulness checks, then external validation

Does it follow its genes?

Does the pathway unit rise and fall with the average expression of the genes assigned to it?

Spearman correlation between the unit and the mean of its member genes, on held-out donors.

Which genes influence it most?

What fraction of the unit’s total Integrated Gradients attribution falls on genes inside its named pathway?

Integrated Gradients attribution purity = Σ|IG| on pathway-member genes ÷ Σ|IG| across all input genes.

What if we remove its genes?

If we zero the pathway-gene inputs, does the unit change more than when we zero the same number of non-member genes?

Ablation ratio = mean |Δz| after zeroing standardized member-gene inputs ÷ mean |Δz| across 20 size-matched random non-member sets.

Lab notebook

Data

  • Kang 2018: 8 donors, control vs IFN-β, 24,562 cells after a ≥200-gene filter. CP10k + log1p.
  • MSigDB Hallmark v2026.1.Hs (Subramanian et al., 2005; Liberzon et al., 2015); a pathway needs ≥10 genes with ≥1% detection in training donors.

Models

  • A gene-set average · B PCA · C unrestricted autoencoder (genes → 256 → K → genes)
  • D unrestricted encoder + masked non-negative decoder; unit k reconstructs only genes assigned to pathway k.
  • E same architecture as D, but gene identities are globally permuted while pathway sizes and the complete pairwise overlap topology are preserved.
  • D2 / A2 D and A with IFN-α/γ split into shared + unique modules

Evaluation

  • 8-fold leave-one-donor-out CV, 3 seeds; filtering and scaling fit on training donors only.
  • Faithfulness: own-gene-set Spearman; Integrated Gradients purity (zero baseline); ablation vs 20 random size-matched sets. Ablation zeros standardized model inputs; it is not a biological gene knockout.
  • Overlap: Jaccard vs |Spearman| of unit scores, all cells and CTRL only.
  • External: frozen Kang-derived representations (final D/D2 models on 2,016 shared genes, seed 0; A/A2 gene-set averages) applied to HIRISA with the Kang scaler; no refitting.
  1. Unrestricted encoder. Labels are hypotheses, so they are tested three ways.
  2. Overlapping Hallmark sets. Pair-level results are descriptive; no test treats the 1,081 pairs as independent.
  3. One Kang perturbation (IFN-β). Specificity claims rely on the external test only.
  4. Five external donors. A donor-count + median rule rather than p-values.
  5. Cross-platform transfer. Within-donor contrasts and Kang reference SDs; absolute levels are not compared.
  6. One final neural seed for the external models.
  7. Hallmark-only input universe. Findings hold within that gene space.
  8. Cell-type differences in stage 2 are descriptive; no mechanism is inferred.
  9. IG depends on its baseline, and zeroing genes perturbs the model; neither is a biological knockout.
